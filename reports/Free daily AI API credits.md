# Free premium AI keys are mostly a myth

No legitimate provider gives free daily or monthly tokens for flagship Claude, GPT and Gemini models through a plain API key, and the closest thing, Google's Gemini free tier, is limited to Flash-class models. As of 29 September 2026, the practical zero-cost stack for a hobbyist in India or Pakistan is a set of card-free tiers: Gemini Flash and Flash-Lite, Mistral's Experiment plan, Groq, Cloudflare Workers AI, OpenRouter's `:free` models and Kilo's anonymous gateway, most of them OpenAI-compatible. Genuine Claude and GPT access needs a small prepaid balance (about $5 minimum, reported by secondary sources) and an internationally enabled card, or AWS's up-to-$200 new-account credit for Claude on Bedrock. Clean APIs (cleanapis.com) advertises 5M free tokens per month (monthly, not daily) across 33 models including Claude and GPT. It has an anonymous operator, a domain registered seven days before this research, no independent reviews and prices far below official list. Peer-reviewed and preprint audits of the relay category found tool-call injection, credential theft and model substitution, and the labs' terms prohibit resale. Nothing found proves Clean APIs is malicious, but it is unverified, cannot be safe for anything sensitive, and is a poor foundation for a gateway you intend to keep. Use it, if at all, only as a disposable extra lane for non-sensitive tests. All figures below were checked on 29 September 2026 and free-tier numbers change often, so confirm them in each console before relying on them.

## Google is the only lab with a card-free tier, and it stops at Flash

Google's Gemini API is the one first-party offering that hands a hobbyist a real API key with no payment method. The official pricing page lists free tiers for Gemini 3.8, 3.7, 3.6 and 3.5 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite and 2.5 Pro/Flash/Flash-Lite, while Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview is paid-only ([Gemini API pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). The catch is data use: free-tier content "is used to improve our products", and paid-tier content is not, so nothing private belongs there. Google no longer prints per-model numbers in its docs, which say limits are visible only in the AI Studio dashboard, are applied per project rather than per key, and reset at midnight Pacific ([Rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)). A September 2026 aggregator reports about 20 requests per day on the 3.5 to 3.8 Flash models and 500 per day on Flash-Lite ([ScriptByAI](https://www.scriptbyai.com/gemini-api-free-tier-limits/)). That figure is unconfirmed, and older sites still quote stale 2025 numbers. Google also exposes an OpenAI-compatible endpoint at `https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/openai/`, still labelled beta ([OpenAI compatibility](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/openai)), which makes it easy to slot into a personal gateway.

Beyond Gemini, the free lanes are smaller and weaker. Mistral's Experiment plan reportedly offers all models for phone verification alone, at roughly 1B tokens per month and about 1 request per second, but only secondary sources describe it and the official docs I could read do not mention a free tier ([Price Per Token](https://pricepertoken.com/endpoints/mistral/free)). Groq's free plan covers gpt-oss and Qwen3.8 27B at 30 requests per minute, 1,000 per day and only 8K tokens per minute, which rules out long-context agent work ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)). Cloudflare Workers AI gives **10,000 Neurons per day** at no charge, resetting at 00:00 UTC, but its newest large models (Kimi K2.6, GLM 5.2, DeepSeek V4) are paid-only ([Cloudflare pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)). OpenRouter allows 20 requests per minute and **50 requests per day** on `:free` models, rising to 1,000 per day after roughly $10 of lifetime top-ups, but its free list is all open-weight or stealth models, with no Claude, GPT or Gemini among them ([OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits), [free models](https://openrouter.ai/collections/free-models)). Kilo Gateway needs no account at all, at 200 requests per hour per IP, though its free models rotate and may be logged for training ([Kilo](https://kilo.ai/docs/getting-started/using-kilo-for-free)). SambaNova's no-card tier is only 20 requests and 200K tokens per day ([SambaNova](https://docs.sambanova.ai/docs/en/models/rate-limits)). Z.ai's free GLM Flash models are small ([Z.ai pricing](https://docs.z.ai/guides/overview/pricing)).

Several sources that a hobbyist will meet are stale. GitHub Models, once the easiest free route to GPT and Claude-class models, was retired on 30 July 2026 ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-30-github-models-is-now-retired/)). Cerebras replaced its free daily tier with a $5 trial credit that expires in 30 days and requires a verified payment method ([Cerebras](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/support/rate-limits)), yet OpenRouter's own September 2026 comparison still lists both as free. The Gemini CLI free individual tier ended on 18 June 2026 ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/an-important-update-transitioning-gemini-cli-to-antigravity-cli/)).

## Claude and GPT sit behind a card, and the "free" programs mostly don't apply

Anthropic's onboarding requires adding prepaid usage credits and payment details, with no free credit mentioned ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8114531-i-created-a-claude-console-organization-how-do-i-start-using-the-claude-api)). Secondary sites report a one-time $5 trial with SMS verification and about 14 days' validity ([mindwiredai](https://mindwiredai.com/2026/05/06/free-claude-api-credits-2026/)), but Anthropic's pricing FAQ only says "a small amount of free credits" ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), and I could not confirm the trial. OpenAI has no free API tier. Its complimentary-token program requires opting in to share your inputs and outputs with OpenAI and is limited to eligible organizations, so a new account should not expect it. Reported quotas conflict: 250K per day on flagship models and 2.5M on mini models is the most-cited pair, against up to 1M per day on large models in another source ([Price Per Token](https://pricepertoken.com/endpoints/openai/free)). OpenAI's help article returned HTTP 403 during research, so the numbers are unverified, and the tier structure implies a paid balance first. Viral "$50,000 free credits" posts are unsupported by any primary source. Startup and open-source programs are gated on incorporation, investor referral or maintainer status ([Claude for Startups](https://claude.com/programs/startups), [Claude for Open Source](https://claude.com/contact-sales/claude-for-oss)), which rules out most solo hobbyists.

The workable legitimate paths to real Claude and GPT are small. AWS gives new accounts up to **$200 in credits** valid six months, and secondary sources say they can offset Claude on Bedrock ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2025/07/aws-free-tier-credits-month-free-plan/)), though it needs a card and coverage should be confirmed in the Bedrock console. Google's $300 Cloud trial needs a card, covers Gemini on Vertex, and excludes both AI Studio and partner models like Claude ([Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)). Vertex Express Mode reportedly gives Gmail users 90 days of Gemini with no billing, though the source is secondary. Pay-as-you-go is cheaper than most people assume: Claude Sonnet 5.5 costs **$2 input and $10 output per million tokens**, Haiku 4.5 $1/$5, GPT-6-sol $2/$10, GPT-6-luna $0.10/$0.50, and Gemini 3.8 Flash $0.75/$3.75 through 31 December 2026, with 50% off for batch use ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing), [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing), [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). A $5 top-up therefore buys about 2.5M Sonnet input tokens, enough for real experiments.

The subscription promos popular in South Asia do not produce API keys. These include Jio's 18 months of Google AI Pro ([Jio](https://www.jio.com/google-gemini-offer/)), Google's student AI Plus offer, ChatGPT Go free in India, and Claude Max for open-source maintainers. Anthropic states that routing Free, Pro or Max credentials through third-party tools on behalf of users is not permitted and reserves the right to enforce without notice ([Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). Google suspended paying Antigravity subscribers in February 2026 for using OAuth credentials in third-party agent tools ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/23/google_antigravity_compute_burden/)). A personal gateway must therefore use real API keys, never subscription logins.

The South Asia payment barrier is the deciding constraint, and the evidence is thinner for Pakistan than India. India and Pakistan are both on OpenAI's supported-countries list ([Supported countries](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/supported-countries)), but Anthropic bills in USD only, with no INR or UPI route ([AICredits blog](https://aicredits.in/blog/claude-api-india-pay-in-inr), a secondary and vendor-authored source). OpenRouter accepts cards, Alipay and USDC only ([OpenRouter FAQ](https://openrouter.ai/docs/faq)). Google's Prepay minimum is $5 or the regional equivalent, and UPI is claimed only by secondary sources ([Billing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/billing)). Whether Pakistani or Indian local cards work for any of these was not verified, so an internationally enabled Visa or Mastercard remains the practical key. Without one, the card-free stack above is what is left. INR-billing resellers such as AICredits exist but are unofficial and unvetted here.

## Clean APIs matches the profile of an unverified relay

Clean APIs is an OpenAI-compatible endpoint at `https://cleanapis.com/v1` that claims 33 models, including "Claude Fable 5", "GPT-5.6 Luna" and DeepSeek variants ([cleanapis.com](https://cleanapis.com)). Its free plan is **5M tokens per month**, 60 requests per minute and no card, and paid plans run from $3.50 for 50M tokens to a $50 "unlimited" plan, all with access to all 33 models ([pricing](https://cleanapis.com/pricing)). The monthly allowance resets without rollover, and terms stipulate that the remaining balance is forfeited if the account is terminated ([terms](https://cleanapis.com/terms)). Only the site's own pages describe it. The domain was registered on **22 September 2026** ([Verisign RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/cleanapis.com)), yet its terms carry an effective date of 23 August 2026 and a blog post is dated 18 September. That may reflect a domain change or templated text, but it is unexplained. The operator is named only as "Clean APIs", with no legal entity or jurisdiction, upstream providers are not disclosed and no official reseller status is claimed ([privacy](https://cleanapis.com/privacy)), and searches found no reviews, scam-checker pages or community mentions.

The pricing does not reconcile with official rates. At $3.50 per 50M tokens the site charges about $0.07 per million tokens, against official Claude Fable 5.1 pricing of $10 input and $50 output, a gap of roughly 140 to 700 times. Only the cheapest small models, such as GPT-6-luna at $0.10/$0.50 or gpt-5-nano at $0.05/$0.40, approach that price. Either the "premium" names map to cheaper backends, token metering is non-standard, or upstream access is obtained by irregular means, and public information cannot say which. Grey-market relays typically source cheap access through free-trial abuse, payment fraud and pooled or reverse-engineered subscriptions. The Cloud Security Alliance reports discounts up to 97.8% and an Anthropic credit worth $3,333 resold for about $59 ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-llm-api-relay-market-shadow-risk-20260729/)), figures I could not verify independently. The category is also opaque: one study of 3,939 reseller endpoints found 37.1% of tested pairs share an upstream cache, meaning many small brands sit on top of other resellers ([arXiv 2608.20732](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20732v1)). To be fair to Clean APIs, its polished terms, Paddle billing (a merchant of record that offers card chargeback recourse) and Cloudflare front distinguish it from the crude Telegram-style operations, but polish costs nothing to produce and does not verify practice.

## The risks are documented, and the labs' own terms bar resale

The strongest evidence concerns what relays do to traffic they can read in plaintext. Researchers bought 28 paid routers and collected 400 free ones; **1 paid and 8 free routers injected malicious code into tool calls**, 2 used adaptive evasion, 17 touched researcher-owned AWS canary credentials, and 1 drained ETH from a private key ([arXiv 2604.08407](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08407v1)). Clean APIs publishes setup guides for Cursor, Cline, Claude Code, Kilo Code and opencode, so its users are exactly the agentic-coding population that the study shows is most exposed. A separate audit flagged model substitution in **7 of 28 platform-model combinations**, 6 of them on Claude endpoints, with low-priced tiers mismatching about 16% of the time against about 5% for upgraded tiers ([arXiv 2605.29524](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29524v2)). That paid-router share (1 of 28) was small, and the sample is skewed toward free and Chinese-market storefronts, so it shows real risk without measuring Clean APIs specifically. There is no documented incident involving Clean APIs itself, and I did not test the service, so whether it serves the models it names remains unknown. Lifespans in the gray market are short: one Zhihu account puts most small operators at one to three months and reports large-scale disappearances after regulatory action ([Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2073039910156746850)), a single-source claim. The Clean APIs "we do not store your prompts" pledge is unverifiable, since the relay sees everything it forwards.

The terms of the labs make this a structurally fragile business. Anthropic's Commercial Terms bar reselling "except as expressly approved by Anthropic" ([Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)), and its Consumer Terms bar sharing API keys ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). Search-snippet text of OpenAI's Services Agreement, which I could not open directly, prohibits buying, selling or transferring API keys and reselling account access ([OpenAI Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/)). Google's Gemini terms are less explicit on resale but forbid replicating the service ([Gemini API Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). A relay without provider authorization can lose upstream supply overnight, and users then face an HTTP 402 or a forfeited balance rather than a ban of their own accounts. The direct account-ban risk falls on anyone who hands a relay their own keys or subscription logins, which you should never do. The sanctioned resale channels are Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry ([Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)).

If you use a Clean API-style relay anyway, the safeguards follow from the evidence. Send only non-sensitive experiments, and never secrets, personal data or client code. Use a dedicated email and unique password, and prepay small monthly amounts on a virtual or limited card. Turn off auto-approve in coding agents and run them in a sandbox with no cloud, SSH or wallet credentials. Cap spend on the relay key and keep a fallback provider. Verify model identity periodically, using an open-source auditor such as [api-relay-audit](https://github.com/toby-bridges/api-relay-audit) (not evaluated here) or your own long-context and behavior probes, because substitution has been shown to vary over time and by price tier.

## Conclusion

The question assumes a pool of free premium tokens exists behind sites like Clean APIs. The evidence says the legitimate supply is Flash-class Gemini plus small or open-weight models, and everything more capable is either paid, gated by company status, or locked inside the vendor's own tools. A price gap of two orders of magnitude below official Claude rates is therefore not a discount but a question about where the tokens come from, and the gray-market literature suggests the answers are seldom benign. Clean APIs may turn out to be a properly run aggregator, but on public evidence it is unproven and should be treated as untrusted infrastructure.

For a South Asian hobbyist, the best gateway design uses free lanes for volume and a small real balance for quality. Route routine traffic to Gemini Flash-Lite, Mistral, Groq, Cloudflare and OpenRouter free models behind LiteLLM or a similar OpenAI-compatible gateway, and keep the data-use caveats in mind for Google and Mistral. Reserve a $5 to $10 top-up on Anthropic, OpenAI or Google for the few tasks that need a premium model, and pursue an internationally enabled card first, since that single item unlocks the most legitimate options. Two open questions remain worth resolving before committing: the current real per-model limits in AI Studio, and whether Pakistan-issued and Indian domestic cards work for prepaid top-ups at the labs, which none of the sources verified.
