# Relay/reseller AI API sites (cleanapis.com and comparable services): legitimacy and safety, as of 29 Sept 2026

All URLs accessed 2026-09-29 unless noted. Read-only research: no signup, no keys entered, no contact with any service. Note: page contents were retrieved via a fetch tool that summarizes pages with a small model, so paraphrases of terms/policies should be re-read against the live page before being quoted verbatim in a final report. Items marked (unverified) could not be confirmed.

## 1. What is cleanapis.com? Who runs it, what does it offer, what do its own documents say, and what independent evidence exists?

### Takeaway
cleanapis.com ("Clean APIs") is a brand-new (domain registered 22 Sept 2026, seven days before this research), anonymous-operator, OpenAI-compatible aggregator advertising 33 models (incl. Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, Gemma, Qwen) with a free tier of 5M tokens per MONTH (not daily) and paid plans at roughly $0.05-0.07 per million tokens. I found no independent reviews, scam-checker results, Reddit/HN/GitHub/Trustpilot mentions, named operator, or disclosed upstream providers. Absence of evidence is not evidence of fraud, but the combination of a one-week-old domain, no identifiable operator and implausibly low prices is a strong caution signal.

### Cited Findings
- Offer: "unified access to 33 AI models through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint" (base URL https://cleanapis.com/v1); models named on the homepage include "Claude Fable 5", "GPT-5.6 Luna", DeepSeek-V4 variants, Gemma 2, Qwen, Seed 2.1 Turbo. I could not independently verify these model names against the providers' official model lists, and the /models page renders its table client-side so I could not read the per-model price table. — [cleanapis.com](https://cleanapis.com), [cleanapis.com/models](https://cleanapis.com/models)
- Pricing (site's own words): Free $0 = 5M tokens/month, 60 req/min, no card; Starter $3.50 = 50M; Basic $5.75 = 100M; Pro $9.50 = 200M; Scale $15.50 = 500M; "Unlimited" $50 (rate limit 3,000 req/min still applies). The site states every plan including Free has access to all 33 models. Overage billed from prepaid balance at each model's published rate; empty balance returns HTTP 402. — [cleanapis.com/pricing](https://cleanapis.com/pricing)
- It is monthly, not daily: monthly allowance resets and "unused tokens do not carry over" (ToS). Prepaid account balance "does not expire while account remains open" (ToS). — [cleanapis.com/terms](https://cleanapis.com/terms)
- Domain age: RDAP record for cleanapis.com shows registration 2026-09-22T09:56:32Z, expiry 2027-09-22, nameservers Cloudflare (CORA/KONNOR), status "client transfer prohibited". Footer only says "© 2026 Clean APIs". Payments via Paddle (a merchant of record), Cloudflare in front. — [terms](https://cleanapis.com/terms), [privacy](https://cleanapis.com/privacy)

- Data policy claims: "We do not store the content of your prompts or the models' replies"; only metadata (timestamps, token counts, latency, status) kept; truncated provider error messages may be logged; "your prompts are transmitted to the upstream provider ... and that provider's own retention policy then applies"; "We do not use your prompts or outputs to train models." - Independent footprint: web searches for the domain name, "review scam legit", and "Clean APIs" with reddit/hacker news/trustpilot/github returned only the site's own docs/blog pages and unrelated results. I did not find any ScamAdviser/Scamdoc/Trustpilot page for it, nor any community mentions. — [search: cleanapis.com], [search: review scam legit]; the site itself is the only source. Its own pages indexed: [docs](https://cleanapis.com/docs), [blog: best AI coding tools](https://cleanapis.com/blog/best-ai-coding-tools)

### Inferences
- Price plausibility: 50M tokens for $3.50 ($0.07/1M) and 200M for $9.50 ($0.0475/1M), with all 33 models included on every plan including a free tier, is far below what I understand official list prices for frontier Claude/GPT-class models to be (generally several dollars per million tokens; my recollection, not verified in this session against current price pages). Either the "premium" models are mapped to cheaper backends, the token allowance is metered in a non-standard way (e.g., a per-model weighting not visible on the pricing page), or upstream access is obtained by non-standard means. I cannot determine which from public information.
- A 7-day-old domain plus unnamed operator means there is no track record: no evidence either way on reliability. The "Unlimited $50" plan and "free 5M/month" are typical customer-acquisition patterns that also appear in gray-market relays.
- The mismatch between the 23 Aug 2026 policy date / 18 Sept blog date and the 22 Sept domain registration is worth flagging; it may indicate a domain change (which is common among relay operators that rebrand, see section 2) but is unconfirmed.
- The terms and privacy policy are unusually well-written for the category (clear refund, expiry and data statements); that is mildly positive but costs nothing to produce and does not verify practice.

### Gaps
- Whether the site is a legitimate metered reseller, a gateway over OpenRouter-type aggregators, a pooled-key/relay operator, or something else: not determinable from public pages; no upstream disclosure.
- The per-model price table and exact model IDs (the /models page loads data client-side; I did not call the API to avoid contacting the service in a way that could count as using it).
- Whether "Claude Fable 5" and "GPT-5.6 Luna" are real official model names; not checked against providers' official model lists in this pass.
- Operator identity, jurisdiction, company registration, WHOIS registrant (redacted/not shown in RDAP), pre-22-Sept history of the domain, any user reviews, any scam-checker verdicts: none found.

## 2. What comparable "free daily tokens / very cheap Claude-GPT-Gemini" relay sites exist in 2026, and how do they get cheap access?

### Takeaway
The category is large and mostly Chinese-language ("中转站", "公益站"), built on open-source gateways (new-api, one-api, sub2api, claude-relay-service). Low prices come from a mix of legitimate bulk/discounted official credit resale, reverse-engineered or pooled consumer subscriptions ("2API" / "拼车"), free-trial/promo abuse, stolen or leaked keys and payment fraud, and (per audits) cheaper models served under premium names. Lifespans are short. Do not treat any as endorsed; the signals below characterize the category.

### Cited Findings
- Taxonomy of upstream sources in a Chinese developer-community guide (Tencent Cloud Developer Community, 20-21 April 2026): (1) official keys ("pure official"), most reliable; (2) reverse-engineered ("逆向"): "breaking official web/app protocols to convert them into API format", cheap but unstable; (3) subscription conversion ("2API"): converting monthly subscriptions into API format for cost sharing. Typical multipliers 0.8-1.5x official price; suspiciously low (e.g., Opus at 0.1x) indicates quality problems; key risks: model swapping, context truncation, shutdown when reverse channels fail. — [Tencent Cloud Developer Community](https://cloud.tencent.com/developer/article/2657436)
- Cloud Security Alliance research note (29 July 2026) lists four ways relay operators source credentials cheaply: free-trial abuse (automated account creation to claim intro credits), payment fraud (stolen/prepaid cards, chargebacks), "denial of wallet" against exposed endpoints, and harvesting credentials from unpatched servers/compromised software. Reports discounts of up to 97.8% off official rates; an example of an Anthropic credit worth $3,333 sold for about $59 (425 RMB); top-10 relays 3.6M combined monthly visits; a V2EX thread with 35,000 views and 190 replies calling the market a "multi-billion RMB industry". These figures are from a secondary research note; I did not verify the underlying sources. — [CSA Lab Space](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-llm-api-relay-market-shadow-risk-20260729/)
- Sub2API (open source) is described as a one-stop relay that pools subscriptions from Claude, OpenAI, Gemini and Grok behind platform-issued API keys with "拼车共享" (carpool-style quota sharing); it stores upstream `access_token`/`refresh_token`/`api_key` credentials. Its own README disclaimer: "Using this project may violate the terms of service of Anthropic and other upstream providers" and the authors "assume no liability for account bans, service interruptions, data loss...". — [GitHub Wei-Shaw/sub2api](https://github.com/Wei-Shaw/sub2api); companion project [claude-relay-service](https://github.com/Wei-Shaw/claude-relay-service); gateway [QuantumNous/new-api](https://github.com/QuantumNous/new-api)
- Ongoing community worry about bans when running Claude subscriptions through sub2api (LINUX DO thread, ~28 Sept 2026; GitHub issue #2092 asking whether a Claude Code Max account gets banned when run through sub2api on an overseas server). — [LINUX DO](https://linux.do/t/topic/2963473), [sub2api issue #2092](https://github.com/Wei-Shaw/sub2api/issues/2092)
- "公益站" (community "public-welfare" relay sites) advertise sign-up credits plus daily check-in quotas (e.g., one gist lists 8 sites with registration+invite credits and 167-190 daily check-in units) and there are auto-probing directories (e.g., a GitHub Actions site checking 15+ sites every 6 hours). Their own directories warn of "掺水" (watering down/model dilution), "跑路" (running away), and "降级" (downgrading), and advise not to send personal/customer data, not to bind main accounts, not to top up large sums; rule changes, rate-limiting and shutdowns are described as common. — [gist listing free relay sites](https://gist.github.com/wosnxn123/0c4032ee9011d5335937412d38479aa7), [ai-welfare-hub](https://github.com/wynx1123/ai-welfare-hub), [ai-coding-welfare](https://github.com/panxunying/ai-coding-welfare) (I did not open these pages in full; characterization is from search-result snippets, so treat details as unverified)
- Lifecycle/enforcement: a Zhihu article reports that after May 2026 regulatory/police action, six or seven in ten accessible gray-market relays disappeared within three months and over 70% of tracked domains were inaccessible within six months; most small operators last 1-3 months before rebranding; it cites a named operator detained 37 days in Shanghai. Single-source, journalistic/opinion piece; I could not corroborate the case independently (the fetch summary also labelled the article's date framing oddly). — [Zhihu 生死一梦](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2073039910156746850); a second Zhihu piece titled "起底AI中转站：封号跑路，模型降智，倒卖用户数据" ("Exposing AI relays: bans, running away, model downgrading, reselling user data") exists at [zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2047369908347057529](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2047369908347057529) but returned HTTP 503 so I did not read it.
- Criminal end of the spectrum: "silver.inc" ("The Unified LLM API Gateway"), attributed by Pillar Security to actor "Hecker", resold unauthorized access to 30+ LLM providers at 40-60% discounts, hosted on bulletproof infrastructure in the Netherlands, marketed on Discord/Telegram, taking crypto and PayPal; supply came from scanning for exposed LLM/MCP endpoints (35,000 attack sessions captured Dec 2025-Jan 2026). — [Pillar Security](https://www.pillar.security/blog/operation-bizarre-bazaar-first-attributed-llmjacking-campaign-with-commercial-marketplace-monetization), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-hijack-exposed-llm-endpoints-in-bizarre-bazaar-operation/)
- Resale chains are deep and opaque: a prefix-cache side-channel study measured 3,939 reseller endpoints (~1.1M requests) and found 236 of 636 tested endpoint pairs (37.1%) share a cache domain, with a containment structure spanning seven layers, i.e. many resellers sit atop other resellers. — [arXiv 2608.20732](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20732v1) (HKUST/ETH/Zhejiang; posted Aug 2026, preprint)
- Non-relay comparison: legitimate cheap options exist as first-party offerings (provider free tiers, official batch/caching discounts, cloud marketplaces). I did not research these in this pass beyond the Anthropic docs referencing Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud and Microsoft Foundry as supported third-party inference routes. — [Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)

### Inferences
- cleanapis.com's formulation (single OpenAI-compatible endpoint, all models on every plan, generous flat token buckets) looks structurally similar to the gray-market relay category, but it is a Western-facing, English-language, Paddle-billed storefront with polished legal pages; that does not distinguish between a well-run aggregator over paid upstream keys and a relay using cheaper/dubious sources.
- Because of resale chains (37% of tested endpoint pairs share upstream caches), a small brand may not control, or even know, where requests ultimately go.

### Gaps
- No systematic public dataset of "free daily tokens" relays outside Chinese-language directories; I did not identify English-language relay sites by name (deliberately not promoting any).
- Exact base rates (share of relays that are stolen-key vs. legitimate bulk) are not established by reliable primary data.

## 3. Do the major labs' terms prohibit reselling/sharing API access or routing consumer subscriptions through an API?

### Takeaway
Yes for Anthropic and OpenAI, explicitly; Google's Gemini API terms are less explicit about resale/key-sharing but prohibit replicating/extracting the service and restrict developer use to authorized contexts. Anthropic also explicitly prohibits routing Free/Pro/Max subscription credentials on behalf of others, and has technically enforced this. Authorized channels (Bedrock, Vertex/Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry, official APIs, approved resale) are the sanctioned routes.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic Commercial Terms (Section D.4, as summarized by fetch): customer may not "resell the Services except as expressly approved by Anthropic", build competing products/train competing models, reverse engineer or duplicate the Services, or support a third party doing so. — [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Anthropic Consumer Terms: "You may not share your Account login information, Anthropic API key, or Account credentials with anyone else"; also prohibit automated/non-human access except via an API key, and using the Services to develop competing products or "resell the Services". — [Anthropic Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)
- Anthropic Claude Code docs: "Customers may not pay for, resell, or intermediate Claude usage on their end users' behalf" (each end user must use their own API key, subscription credentials, or a 3P provider credential); OAuth is "intended exclusively for purchasers of Claude Free, Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise"; "Anthropic does not permit third-party developers ... to route requests through Free, Pro, or Max plan credentials on behalf of their users"; developers "may not collect, store, or intermediate Claude.ai credentials or session tokens"; Anthropic "may [enforce] without prior notice". — [Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)
- Enforcement precedent: on 9 Jan 2026 Anthropic tightened safeguards against third-party tools (e.g., OpenCode) that spoofed the Claude Code client to use subscription pricing; some accounts were auto-suspended for tripping abuse filters (Anthropic said it would reverse errors). — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-cracks-down-on-unauthorized-claude-usage-by-third-party-harnesses)
- OpenAI Services Agreement (all versions since at least Aug 2023 per a search snippet): customer will not "buy, sell, or transfer API keys from, to, or with a third party" and "may not resell or lease access to its Account or any End User Account". I could not open the primary document (HTTP 403 for openai.com; the PDF at cdn.openai.com/osa/openai-services-agreement.pdf could not be parsed here), so this is quoted via search-engine text; verify against the primary. — [OpenAI Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/), [PDF](https://cdn.openai.com/osa/openai-services-agreement.pdf)
- Google Gemini API Additional Terms (effective 23 March 2026 per the fetch): "You may not use the Services to develop models that compete with the Services"; "You also may not attempt to reverse engineer, extract or replicate any component of the Services"; access only within available regions; the Gemini API/AI Studio is "for developers building with Google AI models for professional or business purposes, not for consumer use". The fetch found no explicit resale or key-sharing clause on this page (Google's broader Cloud/APIs terms may contain one; not checked). — [Gemini API Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)
- Sub2API's own README concedes ToS violation risk (see section 2). — [sub2api](https://github.com/Wei-Shaw/sub2api)

### Inferences
- A relay that sells access using pooled consumer-subscription OAuth tokens, or that resells official API keys without a contractual reseller agreement, breaches these terms on the operator's side; the end user is normally not the contracting party with the provider, so the main direct consequences for users are indirect (service cut-off, loss of balance), and account bans mainly hit people who supply their own provider account/credentials to a relay. Bans of the user's own official accounts are a risk if you give a relay your subscription login or key.
- Resale can be legitimate only with provider authorization (Anthropic: "except as expressly approved") or via first-party cloud marketplaces that have their own agreements.

### Gaps
- Primary OpenAI clause text not directly read (403 / unparsable PDF). Google's broader API ToS on key sharing not checked.
- Provider position on whether a user who merely buys from a relay (not supplying credentials) violates terms: not addressed in the sources I read.

## 4. Documented incidents 2025-2026 (key theft, prompt logging, tampering, substitution, shutdown) and concrete user risks

### Takeaway
There is solid peer-reviewed/preprint evidence that a non-trivial share of third-party relays modify tool calls, touch credentials, or serve a different model than advertised; and documented criminal supply chains that resell hijacked access. Shutdown/exit risk is high in the gray market. There is no evidence in this research of any incident involving cleanapis.com.

### Cited Findings
- "Your Agent Is Mine" (arXiv 2604.08407, 9 Apr 2026; accepted to ACM CCS Oct 2026): authors bought 28 paid routers (Taobao, Xianyu, Shopify storefronts) and collected 400 free routers built on sub2api/new-api templates. Findings: 1 paid and 8 free routers actively injected malicious code into tool calls; 2 used adaptive evasion (e.g., activating only after 50 prior calls, or only when not in YOLO mode); 17 touched researcher-owned AWS canary credentials; 1 drained ETH from a researcher-owned private key; weak-router decoys exposed 99 credentials across 440 sessions; a single leaked OpenAI key yielded 100M GPT tokens; routers have full plaintext access to payloads and no provider enforces cryptographic integrity between client and upstream model. — [arXiv 2604.08407](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08407v1); press: [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/04/16/llm-router-security-risk-agent-commands/)
- Model substitution (KBF, arXiv 2605.29524v2, 25 July 2026): a black-box fingerprinting method flagged all 155 economically relevant substitutions in controlled tests with no false positives on legitimate controls; in a six-platform "shadow API" audit, 7 of 28 platform-model combinations were statistically inconsistent with their claimed reference, 6 of 7 on Claude endpoints; low-priced tiers showed ~16% mismatch versus ~5% for upgraded tiers of the same model; GPT-5.4 was consistent everywhere. Platforms were anonymized. — [arXiv 2605.29524](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29524v2)
- IRIS (arXiv 2607.20860, July 2026) provides a budgeted audit for substitution and fractional "routing dilution"; it cites prior work claiming ~45.8% of endpoints fail fingerprint verification and documents silent downgrades and billing deviations across live gateways (I read this only via the paper's summary; the 45.8% is a cited-through figure, not verified). — [arXiv 2607.20860](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20860v1)
- Operation Bizarre Bazaar (Dec 2025-Jan 2026): a marketplace reselling hijacked LLM endpoint access; buyers risk legal exposure for knowingly using stolen resources. — [Pillar Security](https://www.pillar.security/blog/operation-bizarre-bazaar-first-attributed-llmjacking-campaign-with-commercial-marketplace-monetization)
- Other 2026 incidents cited in the CSA note (secondary; not independently verified by me): CVE-2026-7482 "Bleeding Llama" in Ollama (May 2026, ~300,000 exposed servers); CVE-2026-33634 (Trivy supply-chain compromise cascading into LiteLLM, credentials harvested from 1,000+ enterprises, March 2026); 15 malicious JetBrains plugins (~70,000 installs) exfiltrating API keys; 376% rise in credential theft targeting AI services Q4 2025-Q1 2026. — [CSA note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-llm-api-relay-market-shadow-risk-20260729/)
- Data risk: CSA note says proprietary prompts, code and context captured by relays can be used for distillation; a Zhihu piece alleges relays retain commercial contracts and source code (single source, unverified). — [CSA note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-llm-api-relay-market-shadow-risk-20260729/), [Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2073039910156746850)
- Shutdowns/prepaid loss: per the Zhihu article, prepaid balances could not be recovered after service termination and most small relays last 1-3 months; my search for a specific documented "exit scam" where users lost large balances found none, only closures attributed to enforcement or upstream-pool bans. — [Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2073039910156746850); search results summarizing this at [zhihu.com/p/2047369908347057529](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2047369908347057529) title only
- Account bans: Anthropic's Jan 2026 anti-spoofing enforcement auto-suspended some accounts (see section 3). — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropic-cracks-down-on-unauthorized-claude-usage-by-third-party-harnesses)
- Local audit tool exists for checking relays for prompt injection, model substitution, tool-call rewriting and error leakage (community project; I did not evaluate its quality or run it). — [toby-bridges/api-relay-audit](https://github.com/toby-bridges/api-relay-audit)

### Inferences
- Risk ranking for a user of a relay: (1) confidentiality of everything sent (the relay sees plaintext by design, and the operator's no-logging pledge is unverifiable); (2) integrity when used with agentic coding tools (tool-call rewriting; highest severity if auto-approve/"YOLO" mode is on and the tool can run commands or has cloud/crypto credentials in the environment); (3) model substitution (empirical rates of roughly 7/28 platform-model cells in one audit, concentrated on premium Claude endpoints); (4) sudden shutdown or forfeited balance (ToS-stated forfeiture on termination; industry lifecycle 1-3 months for gray-market relays); (5) provider-side account bans if any of the user's own provider credentials are involved; (6) ToS violations by the operator that can cut off the upstream supply at any time.
- The evidence base on malicious behaviour is heavily weighted toward free/community relays and Chinese-market storefronts; the paid share (1 of 28) was small. That shows the risk is real but not universal; it does not measure cleanapis.com.

### Gaps
- No documented incident specific to cleanapis.com (nor any user report at all).
- No named, verified exit scam with quantified user losses in 2025-2026; loss data is anecdotal.
- Independent audit of cleanapis.com's actual model identity was not attempted (would require using the service).

## 5. How can a user tell a legitimate reseller (official partner/marketplace/documented gateway) from a risky one, and what practical safety guidance applies if someone still chooses to use one?

### Takeaway
Legitimate paths are first-party APIs or cloud marketplaces (Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry) and documented gateways that bill at or near list price and name their upstreams. Very deep discounts, anonymous operators, no upstream disclosure, "unlimited" plans and free access to every premium model are risk signals. If a user still proceeds, minimize blast radius.

### Cited Findings
- Anthropic names Amazon Bedrock, Google Cloud (Agent Platform/Vertex) and Microsoft Foundry as supported third-party inference providers, with usage billed under the applicable provider's agreement; approved resale exists only "as expressly approved by Anthropic". — [Claude Code legal and compliance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance), [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Community evaluation criteria in the Tencent guide: check multiplier reasonableness (0.8-1.5x official is normal; 0.1x is suspicious), operator reputation/longevity, small test purchases, keep 2-3 backups; verify with long-context tests and comparing complex reasoning against official baselines. — [Tencent Cloud Developer Community](https://cloud.tencent.com/developer/article/2657436)
- CSA note recommendations: scrutinize unusually deep vendor pricing discounts; enforce hard spend caps; treat API keys as short-lived privileged credentials. — [CSA note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-llm-api-relay-market-shadow-risk-20260729/)
- Community directories for free relays advise: don't send personal/customer data; don't bind main accounts; don't top up large sums; keep spares. — [ai-welfare-hub](https://github.com/wynx1123/ai-welfare-hub) and related lists (snippet-level)
- Researchers' finding that routers can rewrite tool calls implies agent auto-approval should be off when using any intermediary. — [arXiv 2604.08407](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08407v1)

### Inferences (proposed checklist; my synthesis of the above)
Evaluating a service:
1. Identity: named legal entity, jurisdiction, real contact and support, company registration; WHOIS/RDAP age (cleanapis.com: 7 days; no named entity).
2. Upstream disclosure: does it say who serves each model (first-party API, Bedrock/Vertex/Azure, OpenRouter) and claim an official reseller/partner status you can verify on the provider's own partner page? (cleanapis.com: no.)
3. Price sanity: compare per-token price against the provider's official price page for the same model; discounts beyond bulk/batch/cache norms (rule of thumb: much beyond ~30-50% off) need an explanation. Free access to all flagship models on a free tier is a red flag.
4. Terms: does it forbid or disclaim ToS conflict with providers, forfeit balances on termination, cap liability at ~3 months' fees (cleanapis.com: yes to the latter two)?
5. Track record: age, independent reviews (Reddit, HN, GitHub issues, Trustpilot), status page history; treat brand-new sites and sites with only self-authored content as unproven.
6. Payment: card via a merchant of record (e.g., Paddle) offers chargeback recourse; crypto-only or gift-card payment offers none.
7. Verification: run identity checks (knowledge-cutoff/behavior probes, long-context needle test, latency/throughput vs. official) or open-source auditors such as api-relay-audit / KBF-style fingerprints, and re-test periodically (tier-dependent and time-varying substitution was observed).
Using one anyway:
- Never send secrets, credentials, personal data, client/proprietary code, or health/financial data; assume prompts and outputs are readable and possibly retained regardless of policy.
- Use a dedicated email and unique password (no reuse), a separate throwaway account, and a virtual/limited card; prepay small amounts, ideally monthly, and avoid annual plans; treat balance as gone.
- Never give it your own provider API keys, OAuth tokens or subscription logins (bans and key theft); use only keys issued by the relay, scoped and rotated.
- With coding agents: disable auto-approve/YOLO, run in a sandbox/container with no cloud credentials, wallet keys, or SSH keys in the environment, and review every tool call/diff.
